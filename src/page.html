06 — Classes & client experience

The class standard

What each class type is, how every class runs, and the two desk policies that come up most.

Class types

Clients book by class type. The schedule is a promise — teach what it says, every time, at every location, from every trainer.

Classical AF stays rooted in traditional Pilates movement vocabulary, principles, pacing and flow, even though you are not expected to follow the classical order. Programming should feel recognisable and familiar to someone with a classical background, with contemporary variations used selectively rather than driving the class.

It should be appropriately challenging for experienced clients while remaining accessible to all levels through modifications and regressions. Emphasise control, precision, full-body integration and efficient transitions — not athletic conditioning or elaborate choreography.

Our contemporary, athletic Reformer class, and the one with the greatest range for creative programming. Classes may incorporate classical movement but should primarily reflect modern Pilates through varied sequencing, unilateral work, strength and endurance challenges, and more athletic movement patterns.

Pacing can be quicker and progressions more dynamic than Classical AF, while the base programming stays accessible to all levels. Creativity is encouraged, but movement should remain purposeful, Pilates-informed, and centred on effective use of the Reformer rather than complexity for its own sake.

A full-body, all-level Reformer class built around multiple jumping series using the Jumpboard or Cardio Tramp, depending on studio equipment. Each class should feature several distinct jump sequences — supine, side-lying, quadruped or other appropriate orientations — rather than treating jumping as a brief add-on.

Use classical and contemporary Reformer movement between jump series to create balance, muscular variety and recovery while keeping a cohesive full-body workout. It should feel athletic and dynamic, but stay controlled, Pilates-informed and accessible through spring changes, range adjustments, tempo modifications and lower-impact options.

A full-body, all-level class centred on stretching, mobility, flexibility and gentle movement, using the Reformer as the primary apparatus. It should feel engaging and purposeful without the physical demand of a traditional workout class, emphasising breath, grounding, control and ease of movement.

Slower transitions, supported positions and sustained stretches all work, but the class stays distinctly Reformer-based rather than becoming a mat, yoga or meditation class. Calm, accessible and physically restorative — without becoming overtly spiritual, philosophical or passive.

Our intermediate-advanced contemporary format. It should feel distinctly more demanding than OG Reformer. Programming may assume greater body awareness, strength, endurance, coordination and familiarity with the Reformer, so you can begin at a higher baseline and progress movements further.

Incorporate stronger resistance, sustained muscular work, more challenging stability and balance demands, advanced progressions and efficient transitions while maintaining good movement quality. Modifications should always be available when needed, but do not reduce the class to an all-level experience because a less-experienced client is present.

An intermediate-level, full-body class combining contemporary OG-style Reformer programming with short bursts of higher-intensity Tabata movement.

Include multiple Tabata segments of roughly three minutes between Reformer sequences — about 9–10 minutes of the class in total — with around 35 minutes of Reformer work including the warm-up and cool-down. The Tabata portions should meaningfully elevate heart rate and create a cardiovascular challenge; the Reformer segments stay athletic, purposeful and distinctly Pilates-based. It should feel energetic and cohesive rather than like two separate workouts.

Run of show

Every class is 45 minutes, start to finish.

−15 minPre-class

Be ready before clients enter

  • Set the studio. Music on, lighting adjusted, and any needed props or equipment prepared before clients begin arriving.
  • Review the roster in advance. Know who is attending and identify first-time clients before class begins.
  • Greet every client. By name wherever possible, whether it is their first class or their 200th.
  • Orient new clients before class. Familiarise first-timers with the Reformer, the studio setup, and any essential equipment or safety expectations.
  • Address limitations individually. Check in privately about injuries, restrictions or movement limitations — not by asking the room collectively at the start of class.
0:00 — 45:00During class

Teach the class that is on the schedule

  • Keep the Reformer central. Mat work may be incorporated selectively, but stays a light feature rather than a substantial portion of class.
  • Prioritise flow and efficient transitions. Thoughtful sequencing, spring changes, prop use and transitions that keep class moving smoothly and intentionally.
  • Progress challenge appropriately. Build difficulty logically and give clients room to progress, without making complexity or intensity the starting point unnecessarily.
  • Keep warm-up and cool-down concise — roughly six minutes combined, leaving the majority of class for substantive programming.
  • Modify appropriately, within reason, balancing the needs and experience of the full group.
  • Challenge clients while reading the room. Encourage effort and intention while staying aware of individual needs and the overall group experience.
  • Motivate without overstepping. Encouragement, energy and positive reinforcement are welcome. Avoid life coaching and overtly spiritual, philosophical or belief-based instruction.
45:00Closing & after

Do not skip the last two minutes

  • Thank the class out loud, before anyone begins leaving the room.
  • Encourage client feedback. When appropriate, remind clients how to share feedback and leave positive reviews.
  • Remain available for questions. Stay on the studio floor for several minutes after class. Do not immediately disappear into the back or leave the studio.
  • Discuss next steps with eligible clients. First-timers and anyone on an introductory offer should be asked what comes next. Know the current offers well enough to speak about them confidently and accurately.

07 — Studio maintenance

Opening, closing & upkeep

The studio should look the same to the 7am client and the 7pm client. If something needs attention, post it — do not leave it for whoever is on next to discover.

Opening
  • Lights and music on — always return the key to the Master Lock.
  • Adjust the AC if needed.
  • Self check-in ready.
Closing
  • Lights off or dimmed, music off.
  • Lock the doors — always return the key to the Master Lock.

Routine maintenance

Lobby & front desk
  • Coffee station stocked — pods, cups and lids, creamers, sugars, stirrers.
  • Water cups out by the stations.
  • Front desk and seating areas tidy.
Reformer studio
  • Spot clean the floors.
  • Props neatly arranged in their spots.
  • Mats returned under the reformers if used.
Bathrooms
  • Paper towel holders full.
  • Toilet paper stocked.
  • Sink areas and floors clean.
Trash
  • Replace full trash bags.
  • Take the trash to the dumpster.
Studio issues

Post anything that needs attention

This logs straight into the STUDIO ISSUES tab in the events workbook, so it is written down the moment you notice it rather than after your shift. Got a photo? Send it to Charley — it usually explains more than the words do.

Broken equipment: move the client first, log the reformer number the same day, and flag the spot so it cannot be booked.
